Across 334,211 observable labelled flight segments on 12 days, uncorrected ERA5 physics (B1) predicted 10,076 persistent contrails against 1,564 observed by Meteosat-9 (ratio 6.44, 95 % day-bootstrap interval 4.92–12.12); H1 is not supported. The interval lies entirely above 1: uncorrected physics predicts *more* persistent contrails than the satellite detects, the opposite of the hypothesis. Correcting the observations for detection efficiency (0.16, the share of CoCiP-persistent segments meeting Driver et al.'s thresholds) gives a ratio of 1.05 (95 % CI 0.81–1.91); this secondary definition does not decide H1. The verdict is unchanged across the label-rule, age-window and tolerance variants (y_k1: not supported, y_k3: not supported, y_age_20_90: not supported, y_age_40_120: not supported, y_tol_ang15: not supported, y_tol_ang30: not supported, y_tol_dist1p5: not supported, y_tol_dist3: not supported). Two caveats bound the result: about a third of the satellite labels are expected to be chance matches (Phase 5 wrong-day check), which inflates the observed count, and almost no labels exist for June–October, when monsoon cloud hides contrails.
