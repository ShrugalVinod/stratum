## 2026-10-15 — Phase 2 trajectory details (implementation, not pre-registered values)
- **Implausibility filter (step 3).** Rates are computed between consecutive reports of one flight. Pairs < 2 s apart are not rate-checked, because 25-ft altitude quantisation makes 1-s pairs look like > 1,000 ft/min. A point whose incoming and outgoing transitions both break a limit is a spike and is dropped. A lone bad transition drops whichever end is further from the 9-report running median (altitude in 1,000-ft units, speed in 50-kt units). At most 3 passes. The thresholds (6,000 ft/min, 100 kt) are the locked ones.
- **What counts as a gap (step 6).** Any interval between reports longer than one resample step (60 s) is a gap. A gap of ≤ 10 min is filled along the great circle at the pre-gap altitude (`filled=True`). A gap of > 10 min splits the flight into pieces `<flight_id>.1`, `.2` … The 10-min value is the locked one.
- **Waypoints on whole UTC minutes**; a segment joins two consecutive waypoints of one piece. Spherical Earth, R = 6371 km.
- **Phase 2 flight sample.** The January 2026 development month has one free ADS-B Exchange day (2026-01-01), so the "January corridor month" for Phase 2 means that day's flights with January ERA5.

## 2026-10-15 — Phase 2 physics and aircraft details
- **Position jumps.** An interval between reports whose distance exceeds 750 kt × Δt + 3 km is a position jump. It is never gap-filled, and the flight is split there. On 2026-01-01 this changes flight pieces from 8,501 to 8,750 and removes impossible > 1,000 kt segments.
- **TAS QC flag.** `qc_tas_ok` marks 100–320 m/s. It is flagged, not filtered, and counted in the run summary.
- **OpenAP synonyms.** Types OpenAP models only via its synonym table (e.g. B77L) and performance calls for types lacking a native drag polar (e.g. A21N) use `use_synonym=True`. This is logged as `openap_synonym` per flight.
- **Type source.** OpenSky DB snapshot first. The ADS-B Exchange type field is the logged fallback (2026-01-01: 1,583 DB, 120 ADS-B, 4 none; 16 DB/ADS-B disagreements, DB wins).
- **Tropopause.** WMO criterion. The 2-km test starts at the bottom of the first layer with Γ ≤ 2 K/km; the reported height is the interpolated Γ = 2 K/km crossing (Reichler et al. 2003). Synthetic ERA5-level profiles: within 0.3 km and 6 % in pressure.
- **Interpolation.** STRATUM features and SAC flags use log-p interpolation (pre-registered). CoCiP uses pycontrails' internal interpolation, which is not changed.
- **B3 behaviour (checked).** The pycontrails latitude correction lowers sub-saturated RHi slightly (≤ 3 % at 10–37°N) and boosts supersaturated RHi strongly (1.12 → 1.16–1.33). B2 divides RHi by 0.95 (+5.26 %).
- **Corridor flights.** A flight is in the corridor sample if any cruise segment midpoint lies inside the 75 km DEL–BOM polygon. The whole flight is kept for CoCiP. On 2026-01-01: 1,706 flights, many crossing the corridor rather than flying DEL↔BOM.

## 2026-10-15 — Tropopause: profile-top boundary + Monte Carlo N = 100 (D-001)
- **Tropopause.** On 2026-01-01, 13,361 of 131,661 segments (10 %) had no tropopause. Cause: in high tropical columns the first stable layer starts at 70 hPa, only ~1.95 km below the top ERA5 level (50 hPa), so the 2-km WMO window could not be evaluated, although the air above warms strongly (e.g. −7.6 K/km). Fix: when the window runs past the profile top, the rule is applied to the available depth if it is ≥ 1.5 km. On the 15 Jan test day, columns without a tropopause fell from 2.2 % to 0 %; tropical tropopause median 96 hPa, northern 180–320 hPa. A regression test uses the real column.
- **N = 100** Monte Carlo members via deviation D-001 (team OK 15 Oct). Convergence is now checked at N = 100 vs 200. `build-segments` and `run-cocip` gained `--force` to recompute finished days once.

## 2026-10-15 — Monte Carlo lower tail (p05) not stable at N = 100; keep N = 100 (Team OK: S. Vinod, 15 Oct, chat "ok p05")
**Found:** on 2026-01-01 (B1, nested members, 73 segments with EF), the N = 100 vs 200 check changed the summed EF by: p50 0.9 %, p95 5.6 %, p05 49.7 %. N = 20 vs 40 gave p05 27.8 %. With Monte Carlo noise alone, the p05 change should shrink about 2.2× from N = 20 to N = 100, but it grew. Most segments make a persistent contrail in only some members, so the 5th percentile falls at the edge of the EF = 0 members and jumps when a few members switch. The summed p05 is only 10–15 % of the p50 sum; measured against the p50 sum, the change is 5.1 %.
**Decision (Team OK 15 Oct):** keep N = 100 (D-001). A larger N would not fix a quantile that sits at a point mass, and N = 1000 would cost about 60 h of Colab. Report per-segment p05 with a note that the lower tail is unstable. Before Phase 7, settle how the decision layer's lower bound is computed, e.g. as the share of members with a persistent contrail times conditional EF percentiles. No locked value changes.

## 2026-10-15 — Phase 3 detector design (provisional; not pre-registered)
Classical line-filter detector (build prompt §3.1), settings in `configs/pipeline.yaml` → `detector:`. Developed on synthetic scenes and on 3 real slots of the development month (2026-01-15 00:00–00:30, which have no flights).
- **Background:** 21-px median, taken on a 3× downsampled image for speed. **Noise:** 41-px box mean of |anomaly| × 1.2533. The detected field is the normalised BTD plus 0.5 × the cold T12.0 anomaly, normalised again.
- **Filters:** 16 orientations × lengths 11 and 21 px. Gaussian core σ = 0.7 px with negative flanks at ±2.5 px, zero mean and unit norm, so the response is in noise units.
- **Ridge thinning:** non-maximum suppression across the line, then hysteresis at 4.5/7.0 on ridge pixels, 8-connected. The first try, hysteresis on the unthinned response at 2.5/4.0, made strong lines 5 px wide and let noise through. Joining pixels through a 3 × 3 dilation glued the side ridges of strong lines onto them, so it was removed.
- **End trimming:** each end is cut where the along-line image (7-sample running mean) falls below 1.5. The filter response runs ~L/2 past a line's true end, which made lines 15–20 % too long.
- **Object tests:** length ≥ 10 px (= pre-registered labelling rule 1); width ≤ 3 px; quadratic-fit residual ≤ 1.2 px; orientation spread ≤ 15°; ≤ 50 % of pixels within 2 px of a Natural Earth coastline.
- **Cloud edges:** the median background keeps edges sharp, so a cloud edge leaves a thin, symmetric line in the background-removed field. Objects are therefore also tested on raw BTD: |left − right flank| / (core − mean flank) ≤ 1.5, with flanks at ±4 px.
- **VZA:** no detection above 65°. Phase 5 observability uses 60°.
- **Confidence:** provisional logistic weights, chosen so that only long, strong, isolated lines score high (on 2026-01-15, 145 of 381 objects ≥ 0.5). They are refitted on the labelled tuning half in Phase 5; the operating threshold stays 0.5 until then.
- **Results:** synthetic recall is 100 % at SNR ≥ 2 (97 % at 1.5) with 0 false objects in 30 noise-only scenes; the cirrus-like blob and the line along a cloud edge are rejected. On real data, two long Arabian Sea contrails are tracked across all 3 slots (orientation 53–56°, confidence 0.55–0.97). Speed is ~1 s per full slot, so ~2 min per day of 96 slots.
- **Known limitation:** linear cirrus streaks and some cloud-edge fragments over NE India and Pakistan are still detected. Separating them is the job of the Phase 5 labels and confidence refit, not more hand tuning on unlabelled data.

## 2026-10-18 — Decision-layer implementation choices (before any decision-layer number)
- **Flags** = persistent contrails under the corrected humidity *with positive EF* (from the corrected central CoCiP run). Found on the local 15 Jan test case: a daytime contrail (net cooling) made the rule "reward" moving to a level with an even stronger cooling contrail. Avoidance targets warming contrails only; ΔEF itself is still the full planned − alternative difference.
- **Portions**: consecutive flagged segments of one flight piece, merged across gaps ≤ 10 min. Transitions: climb 1,000 ft/min, descent 1,500 ft/min, before and after the portion. Ceiling = OpenAP type ceiling; floor FL250.
- **Fuel**: planned and alternative fuel flow from the same OpenAP `enroute` call (same Mach via the ISA speed of sound, same ground track and times), so only altitude and vertical speed differ. A first version compared OpenAP against the stored fuel flow and gave spurious savings; fixed before any real run. OpenAP often makes a higher level slightly cheaper, so the alert *without credit for a fuel saving* is stored and reported beside the pre-registered alert.
- **Only the changed stretch is run through CoCiP** (planned and alternatives cropped identically). CoCiP has no contrail–contrail interaction, so the EF of unchanged segments cancels exactly in planned − alternative. This keeps 100 members affordable.
- **Monte Carlo pairing**: member 0 = central; members 1…100 each pair one humidity draw (band s refitted on a day-bootstrap of the fold's training rows), one CoCiP parameter draw (`CocipUncertaintyParams(seed + m)`) and one efficacy draw (Q5), identical for the planned flight and every alternative.
- **Confidence note** cell counts: labelled examples in the (season, latitude band, FL band, VZA band, day/night) cell from *other days* (a held-out season has no training rows by construction, so counting training rows only would mark every segment "extrapolating").

## 2026-10-18 — Detection efficiency inside the humidity correction (Team OK: S. Vinod, chat "Option 2")
- **Question.** The labels mark contrails Meteosat *could see*; Phase 6 estimated that only 16 % of CoCiP-persistent contrails meet Driver et al.'s detectability thresholds. Fitting the option-(b) scaling factor to those labels as written in the build prompt, y ~ Bernoulli(σ((RHi/s − 1)/τ)), makes σ describe *visible* contrails, so the fitted s would "dry" ERA5 until physics matches only what the satellite sees, and the corrected EF (H3, H4, decisions) would miss most real contrails.
- **Decision (Option 2).** For the correction fed back into the physics, fit y ~ Bernoulli(ε · σ((RHi/s − 1)/τ)), with ε = the fold's training-row detection efficiency (same definition as the H1 secondary). σ then describes persistent contrails, which is the conversion the paper's §4.5 says the detection efficiency is for. In the decision-layer Monte Carlo, ε is recomputed on every day-bootstrap draw and s refitted with it, so detection efficiency (ii) and humidity (i) uncertainty travel together. The literal form (ε = 1) is fitted too and reported as a sensitivity analysis.
- **Disclosure.** Chosen with knowledge of the Phase 6 results (efficiency-adjusted H1 ratio 1.05), which suggest Option 2 will leave humidity nearly unchanged while the literal form would push s towards its 1.20 bound. No locked value changes (the likelihood is in the build prompt, not in `preregistered.yaml`), so it is not a deviation; it is stated in the paper.
- **(a)-vs-(b) rule** is applied to this option-2 (b) (`stratum fit-correction`, 07c step 1) against 07a's calibrated boosted model. 07a's own "(a) vs (b)" line uses the literal (b) and is reported, not binding. H2 is unaffected (it scores the calibrated boosted model against observed labels).
